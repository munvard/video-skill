# Mix styles

Use as many as the film needs. A one minute film can pass through six worlds. What keeps it one film is not the number of styles but the one thing that never changes.

## Give every style a job
| Job | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| Spine | What stays from frame 0 to the end? One object, character, sound, color, question or camera. |
| Story | In what order does the viewer learn? (for example myth and turn, wait for it) |
| Worlds | What does each place look like? Each world can be a different style. |
| Motion | How do things move? Choose one law for the whole film, or let it change at the turn only. |
| Type | One voice for words, everywhere. |
| Sound | One family of sounds, everywhere. |

Write the jobs in one table before the frame plan. If two styles fight for the same job, remove one.

## Ways that work
- **Change at the turn.** The film is paper until the idea arrives, then it is glass. The change of style is the message.
- **A world per act.** The spine passes through, and every join is a shape, a screen or a place they share.
- **One inside another.** A hand-drawn lesson on a real slate, a pixel world inside a 3D computer, a map printed on paper.
- **Contrast of sound and picture.** A quiet film with the sound of an epic, or the opposite.
- **One guest.** A whole film in one style and a single beat from far away, at the peak.

## Signs that it broke
- A stranger says "it changed video".
- Each world has its own font, ease and music.
- A style is there because it is in the library.
- Remove one style: if nothing is lost, it should go.

Build the join between the two furthest styles first. If that join is beautiful, the film works.
